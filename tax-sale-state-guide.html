Know what the state is selling
before you invest.

Some states sell a tax-lien certificate. Others sell the property itself after a tax foreclosure or tax-default process. Some counties also offer unsold certificates or unsold properties later. These are different legal products, with different risks, payment rules and paths to ownership.

Research scope: This is a verified starter map of states and representative county systems—not a guarantee that every county in the state offers every strategy, nor a full 50-state legal opinion. The county's current sale notice, governing statute and bidder instructions control each transaction. Always confirm before funding a bid.

3. State strategy map

Filter the state cards by the type of opportunity. The links go to statutes, state agencies or official county offices.

TAX-LIEN CERTIFICATE

Arizona (AZ)

Lien certificateForeclosure-to-deed path

County treasurers sell real-property tax liens. The winning bidder is the one who pays the required taxes, interest and charges and accepts the lowest interest rate. If the lien is not redeemed, the holder may begin a court foreclosure process after the statutory three-year point; a deed is not automatic.

Redemption / rate: Interest is the bid rate. Arizona law sets the bidding framework. Review the 2026 amendments and current county instructions before proceeding.

Bid statute §42-18114Foreclosure §42-182012026 HB 2780
TAX-LIEN CERTIFICATE + TAX DEED PROCESS

Florida (FL)

Lien certificateCounty-held certificate OTCSeparate tax deed auction

The investor first buys a tax certificate, not the home. Bidders compete by offering the lowest interest rate within the statutory ceiling. A holder may apply for a tax deed after at least two years have elapsed since April 1 of the certificate's issue year, but must pay other outstanding certificates and applicable taxes, interest and costs. The Clerk then conducts a separate property sale.

OTC example: Florida §197.4725 allows purchase of county-held certificates after issuance and before a tax-deed application is made. That purchase remains a certificate investment, not an OTC home purchase.

TAX-SALE CERTIFICATE

Illinois (IL)

Tax-sale certificateJudicial tax deed process

The tax sale purchaser receives a certificate and must follow statutory notice steps. The usual redemption period is up to three years; certain vacant non-farm, larger multi-unit, commercial or industrial property may have a one-year period. If not redeemed, the purchaser may seek a judicial tax deed after completing required notices and procedures.

Redemption amount: Includes the certificate amount and accrued penalty using the penalty bid and statutory periods. Do not substitute a simple annual interest estimate.

Redemption period §21-350Amount of redemptionNotice / deed process
TAX-SALE CERTIFICATE

Maryland (MD)

Tax-sale certificateForeclose right to redeem

A tax-sale bidder purchases a lien created by taxes, fees or certain municipal charges—not the property itself. The owner may redeem until the right is finally foreclosed by court order. The certificate holder must file a foreclosure proceeding within the statutory timeframe; the certificate is not the deed.

Rate / premium: The Maryland Tax Sale Ombudsman currently describes the stated certificate redemption interest rate as 8% per year. County sale rules can add high-bid premiums and payment-specific requirements; check the actual county notice.

Maryland Tax Sale OmbudsmanState tax-sale process
MUNICIPAL TAX-SALE CERTIFICATE

New Jersey (NJ)

Tax-sale certificateRate bidding + premium

The municipality sells a tax-sale certificate, which is a lien; title to the property itself is not sold at the certificate auction. Bidders can bid down the continuing interest rate. If bidding reaches the statutory floor, bidders may compete on a premium. A private certificate holder generally must wait two years after the certificate sale before beginning an action to foreclose the right of redemption, subject to statutory exceptions.

Watch: Premium funds have separate rules and are not simply the investor's interest return. Verify the current municipal notice and amendments.

TAX-LIEN CERTIFICATE

Colorado (CO)

Lien certificateCounty-held certificates in some countiesTreasurer's deed process

County treasurers sell tax-lien certificates. The certificate is a lien and does not change property ownership. If unredeemed, a holder may begin the treasurer's deed process after the statutory waiting period (generally three years), with notices, searches and costs. Some counties publish county-held certificates for purchase.

County example: Lake County publishes a county-held certificate list. Douglas County explains that its certificate sale uses a premium auction and an annual statutory redemption-rate formula—so the premium is not the same as interest.

TAX-DEFAULTED PROPERTY SALE

California (CA)

Direct property auctionNo tax-certificate program

County tax collectors sell tax-defaulted property by public auction, sealed bid or certain negotiated sales. In the usual process, property becomes subject to sale after five years of tax default (with statutory exceptions). California counties do not sell tax-certificate investments. The owner's redemption right ends before the sale; do not expect a post-sale redemption window.

CA State Controller auction overviewAlameda County FAQ — no certificates / redemption
TAX DEED SALE

Georgia (GA)

Property tax sale12-month redemption

The winning bidder receives a tax deed, but ownership remains subject to the former owner's statutory right to redeem for 12 months. If not redeemed, the purchaser still has to complete the required notice process to bar redemption before relying on final title and possession.

Sample: A bidder buys at a county tax sale, then tracks the 12-month redemption period, notice deadlines, title work and legal steps. The bidder cannot treat the sale date as immediate unrestricted ownership.

DELINQUENT TAX PROPERTY SALE

South Carolina (SC)

Property sale12-month redemption

South Carolina sells real property at delinquent tax sale, and the former owner or certain interested parties generally have 12 months to redeem. If redemption occurs, the purchaser receives the purchase price back plus the statutory interest. Payment method and sale-day funding are controlled by statute and the county's bidder terms.

South Carolina Code, Chapter 51Statutory redemption sections
TAX-FORECLOSURE PROPERTY SALE

Texas (TX)

Property auctionTaxing-unit resale

Texas tax foreclosure sales sell the real property, not an investor tax-lien certificate. Redemption is generally 180 days for non-homestead/non-agricultural property, and up to two years for qualifying homestead or agricultural property, with statutory redemption premiums. Property struck off to a taxing unit may later be resold by public or private sale under §34.05; this is a taxing-unit resale, not one standardized statewide OTC marketplace.

Texas Tax Code Chapter 34Texas Comptroller property tax guidance
TAX CLAIM SALE + REPOSITORY

Pennsylvania (PA)

Upset / judicial tax saleRepository for unsold property

Pennsylvania's Real Estate Tax Sale Law provides upset, judicial and repository-for-unsold-property processes. An unsold property after judicial sale may be placed in the county repository. The bureau may accept an offer with written consent of the taxing districts, subject to statutory conditions. This is an OTC-style property acquisition—not a Florida-style private tax-lien certificate.

Local examples: Delaware County and Montgomery County publish repository property lists; Dauphin County accepts repository bids; Berks County currently posts its reopening date. Every county's bid rules, approvals and title risks must be checked.

4. “Can I choose what my redemption will be?”

This is an important course question. Sometimes an auction lets bidders compete on the interest rate. That is not the same as choosing any return you want.

What an investor may be able to influence

  • In Arizona and Florida, bidders compete by accepting the lowest interest rate within the statutory framework.
  • In New Jersey, bidders can bid down the interest rate; if bidding reaches the floor, a premium bidding phase may occur.
  • In some Colorado counties, the redemption rate is determined by an annual statutory formula while bidders compete on a premium amount.

What the investor cannot simply decide

  • You cannot arbitrarily set a redemption rate higher than the law or certificate permits.
  • You cannot assume the face amount is the property's market value.
  • You cannot make an owner redeem or force a tax deed outside statutory notices, waiting periods and court/county procedures.
  • A premium paid at auction may not earn interest and may be forfeited under the local rules.
Kid-friendly version: You can sometimes choose how low a reward you are willing to accept to win the tax certificate. The state sets the rules. You do not get to write your own interest rate after you win.

5. What does “face value” mean?

You mentioned seeing amounts around $255,000, $180,000, $51,000 and $500,000. Those numbers need labels before we use them in underwriting.

\$255,000 • \$180,000 • \$51,000 • \$500,000

On a tax-lien certificate, the face amount may represent the underlying tax debt, fees and other amounts paid or secured by the certificate. It is not automatically the house's current value. A tax-deed auction may instead show an assessed value, minimum bid, opening amount, judgment amount, tax balance or estimated market value. Those numbers mean different things.

A platform can organize steps, coordinate providers and help prepare documents, but it cannot waive statutory requirements or make a defective title clean by saying so. Confirm exactly what is included in the agreement, who the attorney represents, who pays legal/title/recording fees, what is recorded, and what title/possession result is delivered.
